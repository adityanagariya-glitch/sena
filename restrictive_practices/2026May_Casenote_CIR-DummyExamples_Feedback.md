# INCIDENT REPORTING - FEEDBACK

Hi Team,

After reviewing the current Incident Report flow within the mobile application, we have completed an operational and compliance-focused assessment of the form structure, usability, and future scalability. Overall, the foundation is strong and the current structure is already well suited for mobile field use; however, there are several important areas that should be considered for enhancement to improve compliance readiness, workflow automation, audit defensibility, and AI integration capability. We understand that some of these will have to be parked for 2nd phase. If there are some simple adjustments that can be amended as suggested below then please do so.

Below is a consolidated breakdown of findings, observations, and recommendations.

## Current Incident Report Structure Identified

The current incident report flow includes the following sections and fields:

**General Incident Information**
- Date of Report
- Select Participant
- Select Support Worker
- Additional Individuals Involved (multi-select)
- Incident Date
- Incident Time
- Location of Incident

**1. Individuals Involved**
- Name
- Role
- Contact

**2. Summary of Incident**
- Free-text summary field

**3. Detailed Description**
- Free-text detailed narrative field

**4. Injuries or Damages**
- Physical Injuries
- Emotional/Behavioral Impact
- Property Damage

**5. Immediate Actions Taken by Support Worker**
- Free-text action field

**6. Witnesses**
- Name
- Contact
- Statement

**7. Follow-Up Required**
- Free-text follow-up field

**8. Reported To (Supervisor)**
- Supervisor Name
- Date & Time of Notification
- Document Upload

**9. Additional Notes**
- Additional free-text notes field

**Final Action**
- Submit button

## Positive Findings

### 1. Strong Sequential Workflow Design

The form follows a logical operational sequence:
- Incident context
- People involved
- Summary of events
- Detailed chronology
- Impacts/injuries
- Immediate response
- Witnesses
- Follow-up actions
- Escalation/reporting
- Additional notes

This aligns well with real-world incident documentation processes and supports readability during reviews and investigations.

### 2. Accordion / Expandable Section Layout

The accordion-based mobile structure is highly appropriate for support workers operating in the field.

Benefits include:
- Reduced cognitive overload
- Cleaner mobile experience
- Easier navigation
- Improved usability during stressful situations
- Better separation between reporting categories

This design approach should remain.

### 3. Separation Between "Summary" and "Detailed Description"

This is an important structural strength.

The distinction between:
- a concise executive summary,
- and a full factual chronology

will significantly improve:
- internal investigations,
- management review,
- NDIS audit readability,
- future AI-assisted analysis,
- incident trend analysis.

This separation should remain and potentially be enhanced further.

---

> **Key Missing Components & Recommendations**
>
> While the current structure captures narrative information well, the system currently lacks several critical structured data fields required for stronger operational governance and future automation. We wanted to highlight these fields in hopes that they would be easily integrated prior to launch, but if not we'll need to discuss it being updated post launch.

---

## 1. Incident Severity Classification (High Priority)

Currently no visible severity classification exists.

Recommended addition:

**Field:**
- Incident Severity

**Suggested options:**
- Low
- Medium
- High
- Critical

OR alternatively:
- Near Miss
- Incident
- Reportable Incident
- Critical Incident

This is essential for:
- automated escalation,
- notification workflows,
- dashboard reporting,
- filtering,
- compliance categorisation,
- AI-assisted risk analysis.

Without structured severity classification, future automation becomes significantly harder.

## 2. Incident Type / Category Classification (High Priority)

Currently no visible incident categorisation exists.

Recommended addition:
- Incident Category (multi-select)

Suggested examples:
- Behavioural incident
- Medication issue
- Restrictive practice
- Injury
- Absconding
- Allegation
- Abuse or neglect
- Property damage
- Police involvement
- Hospitalisation
- Environmental hazard
- Staff misconduct

This is extremely important for:
- NDIS reporting requirements,
- analytics,
- pattern detection,
- compliance filtering,
- reporting dashboards,
- AI categorisation,
- reportable incident identification.

## 3. Immediate Safety & Risk Status

Currently there is no structured assessment of ongoing risk.

Recommended additions:
- Is there ongoing risk? (Yes/No)
- Participant currently safe? (Yes/No)
- Staff currently safe? (Yes/No)
- Emergency services required? (Yes/No)

This improves:
- operational response,
- escalation logic,
- supervisor awareness,
- after-hours management oversight.

## 4. Restrictive Practice Detection Fields (Critical Future Requirement)

Given the project direction regarding AI-assisted restrictive practice analysis, this should be considered a major future-proofing requirement.

Recommended additions:
- Restrictive Practice Used? (Yes/No)
- Restrictive Practice Type
- Was it authorised in BSP? (Yes/No)
- Was it planned or reactive?
- Duration of restrictive practice

This will become critically important for:
- NDIS compliance,
- reportable incident assessment,
- AI analysis,
- restrictive practice tracking,
- behaviour support governance.

## 5. External Authority Notifications

Currently no structured tracking exists for external notifications.

Recommended fields:
- Police contacted? (Yes/No)
- Ambulance called? (Yes/No)
- Hospital attendance required? (Yes/No)
- Guardian/family notified? (Yes/No)
- Support coordinator notified? (Yes/No)

This is important for:
- audit defensibility,
- escalation tracking,
- compliance review,
- incident investigations.

## 6. Attachment System Improvements

Currently the upload area appears limited to a single "Upload Document" field.

Recommended expansion:
- Upload Photos
- Upload Witness Statements
- Upload Medical Documents
- Upload External Reports
- Multiple attachment support

This would improve:
- evidence collection,
- investigation quality,
- audit readiness.

## 7. Dynamic Field Logic

The form currently appears static.

Recommended improvements:
- If "Witnesses = No" → collapse witness fields
- If "Injury = Yes" → show injury detail prompts
- If "Restrictive Practice = Yes" → show additional mandatory fields
- If "Critical Incident = Yes" → trigger escalation prompts

This would:
- reduce user fatigue,
- improve reporting accuracy,
- improve mobile usability.

## 8. Draft Saving / Auto Save (Highly Recommended)

Incident reporting is frequently interrupted in field environments.

Recommended:
- automatic draft saving,
- recovery after app closure,
- "Continue Draft" functionality.

This is operationally very important.

## 9. Timestamp & Audit Tracking

Currently unclear whether backend audit logging exists.

Strong recommendation:

The system should automatically record:
- report creation timestamp,
- last edited timestamp,
- submit timestamp,
- edit history,
- escalation timestamps,
- approval timestamps,
- user performing modifications.

This is critical for:
- NDIS audits,
- legal defensibility,
- internal investigations.

## 10. Voice-to-Text Capability (Future Enhancement)

Support workers often complete reports immediately after incidents while emotionally fatigued or multitasking.

Voice-to-text support would:
- improve reporting speed,
- increase detail quality,
- reduce worker fatigue,
- improve field usability.

This would be particularly valuable on mobile.

## Overall Assessment

The current incident reporting structure is already a strong foundation from a usability perspective. The mobile-first accordion layout and sequential reporting logic are well suited to frontline support work.

The primary limitation at present is that the system focuses heavily on narrative collection while lacking structured classification and escalation data.

To support:
- NDIS compliance,
- future automation,
- AI integration,
- reporting dashboards,
- restrictive practice oversight,
- risk analytics,
- reportable incident workflows,

the system will require additional structured incident metadata and escalation logic.

The recommendations above are intended to future-proof the module while maintaining the simplicity and usability that already exists within the current mobile design.
