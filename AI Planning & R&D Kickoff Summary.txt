OCR and image upload feature discussion
The team discussed an image-upload flow that must perform OCR to extract text and align values to specific field names, noting AI involvement is needed for key-value alignment.
* Two AI priorities are OCR with key-value alignment and a conversational voice assistant that fills onboarding forms

Project overview — NDIS context and portal scope
Sandeep explained NDIS as Australia’s disability insurance and described the portal’s purpose to serve service providers who deliver services, manage staff, schedule services, and process payments including payroll.

Client onboarding flow and roles within provider organizations
The group reviewed the invitation-based mobile onboarding for participants, role types within organizations (support coordinator, manager, guardian, allied health professional), and states such as invited, in-progress, and pending verification.

AI onboarding assistant and Sena AI chat module
The meeting detailed the planned conversational voice assistant that will guide and verbally collect onboarding fields, behave as an agent that fills forms, and present friendly voice UI; the team also referenced a web chat module labeled Sena AI.
* Service providers must register, subscribe, complete onboarding, and await master admin approval before accessing modules

Chat module, policies and data isolation
The team discussed a chat module intended for employees and service-provider admins to query policies and get updates. They emphasized strict per–service-provider data isolation so one provider cannot access another provider's policy data.
* The chat module will be used by employees and service-provider admins to query organization-specific and NDIS policies, and must restrict access to each service provider's own data.
* Policy and register data must be fetched from the database and may include portal-entered items and uploaded documents.

Sources and structure of policies and registers
Participants clarified that policies may be common NDIS items or provider-specific and can exist as portal entries or documents; the system must fetch these from the database. The team reviewed a library of roughly 20 registers (asset, document control, risk, medication storage, financial reports, participant supervision, etc.) to be surfaced under procedures.

Shift scheduling and mobile staff flow
The organization schedules shifts on the web with client, service, date/time, assigned support workers, to-dos, notes, and costing flags; these appear on the support worker's main mobile screen as upcoming shifts and next shift details.
* Shift scheduling is created on the web and pushed to support-worker mobile screens, including service details, to-dos, flags, and rate-related attributes (e.g., sleepover/24-hour care).

Shift lifecycle, review, and AI past-shift summaries
Support workers review client information and an AI-generated summary of prior shifts before acknowledging and starting a shift, then may pause or complete it and update time entries, breaks, allowances, or reimbursements after completion.
* Support workers will clock in, review client information and an AI-generated summary of past shifts, then pause or complete shifts and submit time entries and optional allowances or reimbursements.
* AI will assist in generating detailed shift case notes from minimal staff input and summarize the case note with highlighted flags drawn from official NDIS guidance.

AI-assisted case notes and flagging
The AI will prompt staff after a shift to create detailed case notes, auto-fill content, and generate a summary with flagged highlights; flagged conditions must reference official NDIS documents to determine when to raise alerts.

Incidents, draft incident reports, and registers
If an incident is indicated in the case note, the system will produce a draft incident report for verification and completion; incident and case-note registers will be viewable per shift and can be completed later by staff.
* If an incident is indicated in the case note, the system will generate a draft incident report that must be verified and can be completed later; incident and case-note registers will be linked to shifts where incidents occurred.

Roster and past-shift dashboard views
The group demonstrated the rosters area and explained that users can view past shift data summarized, including whether past handovers were submitted for specific shifts. The example noted Alexa's past two shifts and an overall summary view intended for web access by managers or support coordinators.
* The rosters/dashboard shows past shift data and summaries for handovers and submission status on the web.

Web vs mobile access and summary availability
Participants clarified that the summary screens and detailed reports are available on the web only and not in the mobile application used by support workers. The team reiterated that support workers only have mobile access while managers/support coordinators access the web summaries.
* The summary and report views are accessible only to managers and support coordinators on the web, not via the mobile app used by support workers.

Reports and report-generation requirements
The discussion covered report types including a monthly overview report and a client-provided document that specifies the required report format. The current screen mock was noted as incorrect and the final report will be populated after approval workflows are completed.
* A client-provided document will define how the generated monthly/overview reports should look for report generation.

AI features: case notes, incident drafting, and insights
The team reviewed AI-related functionality planned for the mobile app, including case-note drafting, insight extraction during case submission, and incident/restrictive-practice drafting. A specific example was the summary shown for shifts that start before submission and recommendation mechanisms tied to these AI features.
* Several AI-driven features were proposed: case-note drafting, incident/restrictive-practice drafting, and insight extraction during case submission.

Compliance, risk detection, and dashboards
Discussion covered compliance audit UIs and registers to validate asset and process compliance. The team described detecting rule violations (for example, stale asset details) and surfacing risks in a dashboard so higher-authority users can escalate and remediate issues.
